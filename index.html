TCP SYN-ACK Hitlist

Opt-out information for network operators

What is this traffic?

You are seeing probe packets originating from the TCP SYN-ACK Hitlist project. We conduct repeated measurements of the routable IPv4 address space to identify responsive addresses. The goal is to construct an Internet-wide IPv4 hitlist: a set of reliably responsive addresses that can serve as representatives for /24 blocks in Internet measurement studies.

All measurements for this project will originate from 192.87.173.77

What do we send?

Our probes consist of:

  • TCP SYN-ACK packets: one probe per destination IPv4 address in each full scan.
  • ICMP echo requests: may also be sent to compare responsiveness and coverage with TCP SYN-ACK probing.

The TCP SYN-ACK packets are not part of an existing TCP connection. Since the target has no corresponding connection state, its TCP stack is expected to respond with an RST packet. We use this response as an indication of reachability, without enumerating open ports or establishing a TCP connection.

One full scan of the routable IPv4 address space takes approximately one week and will be repeated about every month. Probing is spread over this period to keep the traffic volume and operational impact low.

Why are we doing this?

The TCP SYN-ACK Hitlist is a research project run by the Design and Analysis of Communication Systems (DACS) group at the University of Twente, the Netherlands, as part of Pieter van Heijningen's master's thesis in Computer Science.

Internet-wide measurements commonly rely on ICMP echo requests, but these may be blocked by firewalls or other middleboxes. We investigate whether TCP SYN-ACK probing can improve measurement coverage and use repeated measurements to select more reliable representatives. The resulting hitlist can support research on Internet reachability, outage detection, geolocation, and routing.

How to opt out

If you would like your prefix(es) excluded from future TCP SYN-ACK Hitlist probing, please send an email to the address below with the prefix(es) you want excluded.

We will add them to our do-not-probe list, and they will be excluded from subsequent measurements.
